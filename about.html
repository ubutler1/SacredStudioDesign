About

Sacred Canvas Studio & Design is where craft, concept, and spirit meet—built by Uriel Alexander. The work is process-forward, layered, and designed to hold presence in a space.

The Studio

Sacred Canvas Studio & Design is grounded in a belief that art should feel lived-in—like it carries history, energy, and intention. Each piece begins with a direction (a feeling, a truth, a question), then moves through layers of experimentation until the work settles into something honest.

The studio practice centers on material exploration: acrylic inks for flow and atmosphere, acrylic paint for structure and opacity, and oil for depth and finishing. Texture is not decoration here—it’s a language. Light, surface, and distance are considered so the piece evolves as you move around it.

What Guides the Work

  • Intention: each piece starts with a clear emotional direction.
  • Layering: work is built through revisions, glazes, and controlled risk.
  • Presence: the goal is resonance—quiet gravity that anchors a room.
  • Material honesty: texture, edges, and marks are left visible when they matter.

Themes I Return To

Trust & Fear Identity & Becoming Inner Worlds Protection & Vulnerability Energy & Atmosphere Revelation

Materials & Methods

Acrylic inks, acrylic paint, and oil paint are often layered with glazing mediums, flow medium, and retarder for control across different drying times. Where appropriate, mixed media can include collage and metallic accents. Each finished piece is sealed for longevity and photographed with accurate color balance.

How a Piece Comes to Life

  1. Direction — concept, emotion, palette, and constraints are defined.
  2. Build — movement and structure are established with early layers.
  3. Depth — glazing, texture, and contrast refine the visual weight.
  4. Resolve — edges, focal points, and balance are finalized.
  5. Finish — sealing, documentation, and presentation.

Studio Practices

A few standards that keep the work consistent—whether you’re collecting an original or commissioning a custom piece.

Concept Clarity

Work begins with an emotional direction—so the piece has a purpose beyond aesthetics.

Layered Construction

Glazing, revision, and controlled experimentation create depth that photographs can’t fully capture.

Material Exploration

Inks, acrylic, and oil are used intentionally—each chosen for the role it plays in atmosphere and finish.

Process Transparency

Commission clients receive structured checkpoints so the evolution of the piece is collaborative and clear.

Finish & Longevity

Final sealing and care guidance ensure the surface holds up over time and across environments.

Space Awareness

Scale, placement, and lighting matter. Pieces are designed to live in real rooms, not just on screens.
